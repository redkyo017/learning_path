# Resources

A reference page, not reading homework. Come here when you're stuck on something specific,
find the matching job below, use the resource, go back to today's `content/dayNN.md`. Every
link on this page was checked before being listed; two well-known sites are deliberately
left off because they refused connection during that check.

## 1. Start here

The five resources this path already runs on. You don't need anything else to finish the
50 days.

- **[DW Nicos Weg A1](https://learngerman.dw.com/en/nicos-weg/c-36519789)** — the free video
  course used for every Input block. Free.
- **[Goethe Start Deutsch 1 exam page](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/kup/prf/prf/sd1.html)** —
  official exam format, centres, booking. Free to browse; the exam fee is paid at booking.
- **[Goethe A1 practice materials](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/kup/prf/prf/sd1/ueb.html)** —
  the official Übungssätze (mock papers) and the Wortliste that bounds this whole path. Free.
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/)** — spaced-repetition flashcards, run every day in Block
  1. Free on desktop; paid on iOS.
- **[italki](https://www.italki.com)** — community tutors for live speaking from week 3 on.
  ~$8–10/hr.

## 2. When you need to know how a word sounds

**[de.wiktionary.org](https://de.wiktionary.org)** — search any German word here. Each entry
gives you IPA (the exact pronunciation in symbols), a native-speaker audio clip you can play
and repeat, the article (`der`/`die`/`das`), and the plural — everything the Vietnamese-L1
articles problem in `STRATEGY.md` says you must store with the word, in one place. Use it
whenever a word in your Anki deck or a Nicos Weg episode has a sound you can't reproduce from
the spelling alone. For the specific Vietnamese-L1 sound fixes (final consonants, clusters,
sentence intonation), the dedicated drill is `content/PRONUNCIATION.md` — go there first for
systematic practice, come here for a one-off word check.

## 3. When you need to know what a word means

- **[dict.cc](https://www.dict.cc)** — German→English, and shows the noun's gender and often
  community audio. This is your A1 dictionary: fast, bilingual, no wasted time. Reach for it
  whenever a Wortliste word's meaning isn't clear from context.
- **[dwds.de](https://www.dwds.de)** — a German-German dictionary with 600k entries and
  corpus example sentences. Looking a word up *in German* forces you to read more German to
  find the answer, which is a real skill — but it's an A2+ skill. At A1, with under 650 words
  total, using dwds.de as your primary dictionary burns Anki time reading definitions you
  don't have the vocabulary to parse yet. Save it for after the exam, or for the rare case
  where dict.cc's translation feels off and you want the German definition to settle it.

## 4. When you need more practice than the path gives you

- **[schubert-verlag.de/aufgaben/](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/aufgaben/)** — free,
  self-correcting online exercises, including an A1 set.
- **[deutsch.lingolia.com/en/grammar](https://deutsch.lingolia.com/en/grammar)** — grammar
  explanations in English, each with free A1 exercises attached.

A warning before you open either: extra grammar study is the single biggest time sink at A1.
`STRATEGY.md` puts a hard ceiling on this path's grammar for a reason — the exam does not
test case tables, adjective endings, Genitiv, Konjunktiv, or Passiv, and every hour spent on
them is an hour not spent on the Wortliste or on Sprechen output. Use these two sites only
when one specific pattern from a day file — not a whole topic — isn't clicking after the
day's own explanation, drill that one pattern, and stop. They are not for browsing.

## 5. Unverified — check these yourself before relying on them

These are real, well-known German-learning sites, but they could not be confirmed working
from the environment this path was built in (bot protection or JavaScript rendering blocked
the check). They are plausible, not vetted — open each one yourself and judge before you
build a habit around it.

- **[forvo.com/languages/de/](https://forvo.com/languages/de/)** — native-speaker audio
  recordings for individual words, a possible alternative or supplement to de.wiktionary.org
  above.
- **[dict.leo.org/german-english/](https://dict.leo.org/german-english/)** — a German-English
  dictionary with a usage forum, a possible alternative to dict.cc above.
- **[youtube.com/@EasyGerman](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyGerman)** — street-interview
  videos with dual German/English subtitles, potential extra listening input beyond Nicos Weg.
- **[ankiweb.net/shared/decks](https://ankiweb.net/shared/decks)** — shared, pre-built Anki
  decks. Do not substitute one of these for `vocabulary/tier1_wortliste.csv` — this path's
  deck is built to the exact Goethe Wortliste; a generic shared deck won't be.

## 6. What to deliberately ignore

Consistent with `STRATEGY.md`'s mistakes list — these cost time without moving your exam
score, so none are linked here.

- **Duolingo and Babbel** for exam preparation. Streaks optimise for daily engagement, not
  for the Wortliste, and neither app is built around what Start Deutsch 1 actually tests.
- **Buying a textbook stack** (Menschen A1, Schritte International, a grammar reference "just
  in case"). Every textbook paces to a 12-week classroom term and teaches past the exam's
  grammar ceiling. Nicos Weg plus this path's Wortliste already is the complete input and
  vocabulary source.
- **Any resource above A1** before the exam — A2 grammar, A2 vocabulary lists, "bridge"
  materials. That's Tier 3 territory, scheduled for after day 50, not before it.
