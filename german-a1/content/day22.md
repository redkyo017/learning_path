# Day 22 — wollen and möchten

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

`wollen` (want) and `möchte` (would like) both express desire, but only one of them is
polite — and A1 Sprechen and Schreiben both grade politeness in requests. The
10_routine words you finished yesterday also put these two modals directly on the routine
vocabulary you will describe for the next two days: getting up, eating, working.

## Core concepts

`möchte` is already the polite form of "want" — it needs no extra softening word. `will`
(from `wollen`) is blunt and states a plain intention, appropriate between friends or
family but not to a stranger or an official.

- `Ich möchte morgen früh aufstehen.` (I would like to get up early tomorrow.)
- `Ich will heute Sport machen.` (I want to do sport today.)
- `Ich möchte um sieben Uhr frühstücken.` (I would like to have breakfast at seven.)
- `Er will jeden Tag joggen.` (He wants to jog every day.)

With a [trennbares Verb](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) like `aufstehen`, the modal construction
keeps the whole infinitive together at the end — it does not split as it would in a
simple present-tense sentence.

## Exercises

1. Say "I would like to get up early tomorrow" using `möchte`. — **Hint:** `möchte` is already polite on its own — you do not need to add `bitte` to soften it further. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich möchte morgen früh aufstehen.` Check `aufstehen` stayed together as one word at the end, not split into `stehe...auf`.
2. Say "I want to do sport today" using `will`. — **Hint:** `wollen` conjugates as `ich will` — do not confuse it with the polite `möchte` form. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich will heute Sport machen.` Confirm `machen` is last.
3. Say "He wants to jog every day," then check the verb ending on `wollen`. — **Hint:** `wollen` is irregular in the `er/sie/es` form: `er will`, not `er willt`. — **Solution sketch:** `Er will jeden Tag joggen.` Check you wrote `will`, not `willt`.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Using `will` where politeness matters, e.g. saying `Ich will einen Kaffee` to a waiter — this sounds demanding; `möchte` is the polite request form for exactly this situation.
- Splitting a separable verb when a modal is present, e.g. saying `Ich möchte auf stehen` or `Ich möchte stehe auf` instead of keeping the full infinitive together at the end: `Ich möchte aufstehen.`
