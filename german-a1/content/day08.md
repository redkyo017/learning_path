# Day 08 — Yes/no questions and word order

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards finish the time theme and start the home theme, and the exam's Hören section constantly tests whether you caught
a yes/no question correctly in a short dialogue — arrival times, opening hours, whether
an appointment is confirmed. The grammar reflex you need is small but absolute: for a
yes/no question, the verb jumps all the way to the front, ahead of the subject.

## Core concepts

A [Ja/Nein-Frage](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) is a statement with the verb moved to position
one:

```
Statement:  Du kommst um sieben Uhr.
Question:   Kommst du um sieben Uhr?
```

Nothing else changes — no extra word, no "do" as in English. Answer with `Ja, …` or
`Nein, … nicht`:

```
Kommst du um sieben Uhr?  →  Ja, ich komme um sieben Uhr. / Nein, ich komme nicht um sieben Uhr.
```

## Exercises

1. Turn three statements into yes/no questions by moving the verb to the front. — **Hint:** move only the verb — the subject follows it, everything else stays in its original order. — **Solution sketch:** `Du hast Zeit.` → `Hast du Zeit?` / `Es ist spät.` → `Ist es spät?` / `Der Bus kommt pünktlich.` → `Kommt der Bus pünktlich?`
2. Ask a yes/no question with today's time or home vocabulary and answer it both ways, `Ja` and `Nein`. — **Hint:** the `Nein` answer needs `nicht` after the verb and its object, not before the whole sentence. — **Solution sketch:** `Beginnt der Kurs um acht Uhr? — Ja, er beginnt um acht Uhr. / Nein, er beginnt nicht um acht Uhr.`
3. From today's new words (words 126–150, closing theme 03_time and opening 04_home), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** several time nouns look similar in German and are easy to swap under pressure — `die Woche` vs `das Wochenende` — say the full chunk, not just the root. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `die Woche, die Wochen; der Monat, die Monate; das Jahr, die Jahre` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Adding an English-style helper word instead of moving the verb — trying to build something like `"Tust du kommen?"` German has no equivalent of "do" in questions; the main verb itself moves.
- Answering a yes/no question with a W-question-shaped sentence out of nervousness — mixing up the two patterns under time pressure in Hören or Sprechen. Keep the two structures — verb-first for yes/no, W-word-first for W-questions — physically separate in practice.
