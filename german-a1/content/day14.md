# Day 14 — Time expressions: um, am, im

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards finish the health theme and start the work theme, and booking a doctor's appointment — the exact Block 3 task
today — needs all three time prepositions working together: the clock time, the day,
and sometimes the season. Getting the wrong preposition on the wrong kind of time is one
of the most common small errors at A1, and it's entirely fixable by memorising which
preposition goes with which kind of time.

## Core concepts

Three different prepositions, each locked to one kind of time expression — never
interchangeable:

```
um + clock time:    um sieben Uhr, um halb acht
am + day:           am Montag, am Wochenende
im + month/season:  im Januar, im Sommer
```

There's no rule to derive this from — it's a fixed pairing you memorise per category,
the same way you memorised article-plural chunks. Say all three example lines aloud
together until the mapping (clock→`um`, day→`am`, month/season→`im`) is automatic.

**When two or more of them share a sentence, German goes from the largest unit to the
smallest: month, then day, then clock time. The day always comes before the time.**

```
Der Termin ist am Montag um neun Uhr.        ✓ day before time
Der Termin ist um neun Uhr am Montag.        ✗ never this way round
Der Termin ist im Januar am Montag um neun Uhr.   ✓ all three, largest first
```

English lets you say "at nine on Monday", so the reversed order will feel natural and
still be wrong. In a scored Sprechen or Schreiben answer, say the day first, then the
time — always.

Build three time-expression sentences, one each with `um`, `am`, `im`. — **Hint:** ask yourself which category the time belongs to first — a clock number, a named day, or a month/season — then pick the matching preposition, never by feel. — **Solution sketch:** `Der Termin ist um neun Uhr. From today's new words (words 276–300, closing theme 06_health and opening 07_work), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** the nouns in today's slice are all work words, and job titles come in matched masculine/feminine pairs — learn both halves of a pair together, not just the one you would use for yourself. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `der Kollege, die Kollegen; die Kollegin, die Kolleginnen; das Büro, die Büros` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Using `um` for a day or `am` for a clock time, treating the three prepositions as interchangeable "at/on/in" the way English sometimes blurs them. German locks each preposition to exactly one category — there is no overlap to lean on.
- Ordering a sentence's time expressions smallest-to-largest — saying `Der Termin ist um neun Uhr am Montag`, which mirrors the English "at nine on Monday" but is not how German orders them. Largest unit first, always: month, then day, then clock time — `Der Termin ist am Montag um neun Uhr.`
