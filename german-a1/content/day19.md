# Day 19 — Perfekt with sein

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Yesterday's `haben`-Perfekt covered verbs done to an object. Today's `sein`-Perfekt
covers the other class entirely: verbs of motion and change of state — flying, driving,
arriving, departing. Travel narration is almost impossible without this half, and Hören
Teil 2 announcements ("der Zug ist abgefahren") depend on recognising it instantly, not
translating it word by word.

## Core concepts

`sein`-Perfekt has the same shape as `haben`-Perfekt — conjugated auxiliary in
position 2, [Partizip II](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) at the end — but the auxiliary is `sein`,
and it is chosen per verb, memorised, never guessed from a rule.

- `Ich bin nach Deutschland geflogen.` (I flew to Germany.)
- `Wir sind mit dem Zug gefahren.` (We travelled by train.)
- `Der Zug ist um acht Uhr abgefahren.` (The train departed at eight o'clock.)
- `Wir sind pünktlich angekommen.` (We arrived on time.)

Notice `abgefahren` and `angekommen`: the separable prefix (`ab-`, `an-`) stays attached,
and `ge-` slots between the prefix and the stem — `ab` + `ge` + `fahren`.

## Exercises

1. Turn "I flew to Germany" into German Perfekt, then check the auxiliary. — **Hint:** `fliegen` describes a change of location, so it takes `sein`, never `haben`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich bin nach Deutschland geflogen.` Confirm `bin` sits second and `geflogen` is last.
2. Write "We arrived on time" using the separable verb `ankommen` in the Perfekt. — **Hint:** the `ge` slots between the separable prefix and the stem: `an` + `ge` + `kommen` = `angekommen`. — **Solution sketch:** `Wir sind pünktlich angekommen.` Check the participle is one word, `angekommen`, not `an gekommen` or `gekommen an`.
3. Add "by train" to "We travelled" and check the word order. — **Hint:** the participle always stays at the very end, even when you add extra information like `mit dem Zug` in the middle. — **Solution sketch:** `Wir sind mit dem Zug gefahren.` Confirm `gefahren` is still the last word, not pushed earlier.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Using `haben` with motion verbs, e.g. `Ich habe gefahren` instead of `Ich bin gefahren` — motion and change-of-state verbs take `sein`; this is memorised per verb, not guessed.
- Forgetting the participle stays last when extra information is added, producing `Ich bin gefahren mit dem Zug` instead of `Ich bin mit dem Zug gefahren`.
