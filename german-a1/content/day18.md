# Day 18 — Perfekt with haben

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today you meet the [Perfekt](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) tense — the everyday way Germans talk
about the past — through its `haben` half, which covers most verbs: buying, booking,
packing, making. Hören and Lesen both use past-tense narration constantly at A1, and
Sprechen rewards a candidate who can say what they did yesterday, not only what they do
every day. Today's 25 cards also finish the 08_travel theme and open 09_city, the
vocabulary this grammar reflex runs on for the next two days.

## Core concepts

`haben`-Perfekt has two fixed parts in every sentence: the conjugated `haben` in
position 2, and the [Partizip II](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) (`ge-...-t` for regular verbs)
pushed to the very end, however long the sentence gets in between.

- `Ich habe die Fahrkarte gekauft.` (I bought the ticket.)
- `Ich habe den Koffer gepackt.` (I packed the suitcase.)
- `Ich habe den Flug gebucht.` (I booked the flight.)
- `Ich habe im Sommer Urlaub gemacht.` (I went on holiday in summer.)

Every verb above describes doing something to an object — buying, packing, booking,
making — exactly the class of verb that takes `haben`. Tomorrow you meet the other half.

One new small word in those sentences: `der Koffer` becomes `den Koffer` after `haben`,
and `der Flug` becomes `den Flug`. That is the same masculine-only change as `ein →
einen` from day 10, now on `der` instead of `ein`. Memorise the pair — `der Koffer / den
Koffer` — the way you memorise a noun with its article. There is no table to learn, and
feminine and neuter nouns (`die Fahrkarte`, `das Hotel`) do not change at all.

## Exercises

1. Say "I bought the ticket" and "I packed the suitcase" in German, then check both against the haben-Perfekt pattern. — **Hint:** haben-Perfekt = conjugated `haben` in position 2, participle (`ge-...-t`) at the very end. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich habe die Fahrkarte gekauft. Ich habe den Koffer gepackt.` Check that `habe` sits second and both participles land last.
2. Write one sentence with `Urlaub machen` in the Perfekt, then check the auxiliary. — **Hint:** `machen` is a regular weak verb and always takes `haben`, never `sein`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich habe im Sommer Urlaub gemacht.` Confirm `habe` is present — a bare `gemacht` with no auxiliary is not a complete Perfekt sentence.
3. Take one of today's new words — the last of 08_travel or the first of 09_city — and build a haben-Perfekt sentence with it. — **Hint:** pick a verb whose action you did to something — buying, booking, posting — not a verb of motion. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `Ich habe die Briefmarke gekauft.` Read it aloud and check the participle starts with `ge-` and ends in `-t`.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Forgetting `haben` and saying only the participle, e.g. `Ich Fahrkarte gekauft` instead of `Ich habe die Fahrkarte gekauft` — the auxiliary is not optional filler; Perfekt needs both parts every time.
- Misapplying the strong-verb `-en` ending to a regular weak verb, e.g. saying `gebuchen` instead of `gebucht` for `buchen` — regular participles end in `-t`, not `-en`.
