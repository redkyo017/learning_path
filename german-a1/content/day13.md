# Day 13 — Separable verbs

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards are all health words — the food theme closed yesterday — and separable verbs — `aufstehen`,
`einkaufen` and their cousins — are exactly the pattern behind describing a daily
routine, which you'll need heavily from theme 10_routine onward and for Sprechen answers
about your day right now.

## Core concepts

A [trennbares Verb](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) splits in two in a normal sentence: the
conjugated part stays in position two, and the small prefix (`auf-`, `ein-`) breaks off
and jumps to the very end of the sentence.

```
aufstehen  → Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf.
einkaufen  → Ich kaufe am Montag Brot ein.
```

In the infinitive the two pieces are written as one word (`aufstehen`), which is why the
split feels wrong the first few times — trust the pattern, not the spelling of the
dictionary form.

## Exercises

1. Split `aufstehen` and `einkaufen` correctly in two original sentences. — **Hint:** the prefix always goes to the very end of the clause, after every other word, not right after the conjugated verb. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich stehe früh auf. Ich kaufe heute Gemüse ein.`
2. Take the infinitive `fernsehen` and build one sentence with it split correctly. — **Hint:** the pattern is identical regardless of which separable verb you use — conjugate the base part, send the prefix to the end. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich sehe am Abend fern.`
3. From today's new words (theme 06_health, words 251–275), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** health nouns include several compound and borrowed words whose gender isn't guessable from meaning — check the article on the card rather than assuming. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `der Arzt, die Ärzte; die Tablette, die Tabletten; das Rezept, die Rezepte` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Leaving the prefix attached, saying `Ich aufstehe um sieben Uhr` instead of splitting it. The infinitive spelling as one word actively misleads you here — the split only happens once the verb is conjugated into a real sentence.
- Sending the prefix to the end but forgetting to conjugate the base verb, producing `Ich aufsteh um sieben Uhr` instead of `Ich stehe … auf`. Both halves need attention: the base takes the normal ending, the prefix moves.
