# Day 05 — haben, all persons

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

`haben` ("to have") is the second irregular verb the exam leans on constantly — it
carries every possession, every family relationship (`Ich habe einen Bruder`), and every
appointment (`Ich habe einen Termin`). Today's 25 cards are all family words, and from
here on almost every sentence you build needs one of its six forms, so today it becomes
as automatic as `sein` did yesterday.

## Core concepts

`haben` is also irregular, and unlike `sein` it partly matches the regular pattern
you'll meet on day 6 — the `du`/`er` forms just drop a letter instead of changing
completely.

```
ich habe        wir haben
du hast         ihr habt
er/sie/es hat   sie/Sie haben
```

Notice `du hast` and `er/sie/es hat` are not `habst`/`habt` — the `b` disappears. That
contraction is the one part of the [Konjugation](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) that trips
learners who try to derive it logically instead of drilling it by ear.

One thing to memorise rather than work out: after `haben`, a few nouns take `einen`
instead of `ein` — `Ich habe einen Bruder.`, `Ich habe einen Termin.` Treat each of
those as one fixed chunk for now, exactly as you treat a noun glued to its article. The
pattern behind them is explained on day 10; until then, use only the `einen` chunks
given here and say `eine`/`ein` for everything else.

## Exercises

1. Say all six forms of `haben` aloud, in order, without looking at the chart. — **Hint:** only `du` and `er/sie/es` lose the `b` — `wir`, `ihr` and `sie/Sie` keep the full `hab-` stem. — **Solution sketch:** `ich habe, du hast, er hat, wir haben, ihr habt, sie haben` — say the `du`/`er` pair three times in a row, since that's the pair that breaks first under pressure.
2. Build one `haben` sentence for `ich`, one question with `du`, and one statement with `sie` (a friend). — **Hint:** the question form just moves `hast` to the front — no extra word needed. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich habe eine Schwester. Hast du eine Schwester? Meine Freundin hat einen Bruder.`
3. From today's new words (theme 02_family, words 51–75), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** if you can't recall the plural instantly, that card is a leech candidate — flag it in Anki rather than guessing. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `der Bruder, die Brüder; die Schwester, die Schwestern; das Kind, die Kinder` — say each pair aloud twice before checking the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Saying `du habst` or `er habt` by regularising the ending instead of recalling the contracted form. `haben` is irregular exactly where it looks most regular — that's what makes the error persistent.
- Building all ten `Ich habe …` sentences around the same one or two nouns because they come to mind fastest. That drills recall of two words, not production of the verb — force yourself through ten different nouns even if the last few are slower.
