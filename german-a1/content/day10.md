# Day 10 — Akkusativ as a fixed chunk

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards finish the home theme and start the food theme, and `haben` sentences about your home — `Ich habe einen Balkon`,
`Ich habe eine Küche` — need one small article change you haven't met yet. This is your
first brush with the [Akkusativ](GLOSSARY.md#grammar), and per the grammar ceiling in
`STRATEGY.md` you will never see a case table for it — you learn the changed forms as
fixed chunks glued to `haben` and a handful of similar verbs, the same way you already
learned nouns as fixed chunks with their article.

## Core concepts

Only the masculine article changes after `haben` (and similar verbs): `ein` becomes
`einen`. Feminine and neuter stay the same.

```
der Balkon  → Ich habe einen Balkon.
die Küche   → Ich habe eine Küche.
das Zimmer  → Ich habe ein Zimmer.
```

Memorise it as: masculine `ein` → `einen` after `haben`, feminine and neuter unchanged.
Don't try to derive this from a rule about cases — just drill the three example lines
above until `einen Balkon` comes out as one glued word-pair, never `ein Balkon` after
`habe`.

### Block 3 — Output (40 min)

Describe your room in eight sentences, using `haben` with at least three masculine nouns
so you're forced to produce `einen` correctly each time, mixed with feminine and neuter
nouns left unchanged. Write the eight sentences first, check every masculine one against
the `einen` rule, then read them aloud and record the reading.

### Block 4 — Exam drill (30 min)

Open `templates/schreiben_templates.md` and reread the Teil 2 skeleton and worked
examples. Write your own 30-word informal message inviting a friend to see your new
home, covering three points: the invitation, a proposed day and time, and a request for
a reply. Check it against the "three points" rule before checking anything else.

## Exercises

1. Turn three masculine nouns into `haben` sentences, applying `ein → einen`. — **Hint:** only masculine changes — if you're not sure of a noun's gender, check the article on its Anki card before building the sentence, don't guess. — **Solution sketch:** `der Tisch → Ich habe einen Tisch. der Stuhl → Ich habe einen Stuhl. der Schrank → Ich habe einen Schrank.`
2. Build one sentence each with a feminine and a neuter noun after `haben`, confirming the article does not change. — **Hint:** the trap runs the other way too — learners who just drilled `einen` sometimes overcorrect and apply it to `die`/`das` nouns as well. — **Solution sketch:** `die Lampe → Ich habe eine Lampe. das Fenster → Ich habe ein Fenster.`
3. From today's new words (words 176–200, closing theme 04_home and opening 05_food), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** the nouns in today's slice are all food words, and they split across all three articles with no clue from the English translation — `der` Kaffee, `die` Milch, `das` Brot. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `das Brot, die Brote; der Apfel, die Äpfel; die Banane, die Bananen` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Saying `Ich habe ein Balkon` — leaving the masculine article unchanged after `haben` out of habit from the bare `ein`/`eine`/`ein` forms learned for nouns generally. This is the exact chunk today's Core concepts drills; if it slips here, redo the three example lines before moving to the exercises.
- Overcorrecting and changing `eine`/`ein` to `einen` for feminine or neuter nouns too, after drilling the masculine case hard. Only masculine changes — check gender first, every time, before applying the change.
