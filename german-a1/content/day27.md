# Day 27 — dass as a memorised chunk

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

`dass` (that) reports a belief, hope or thought — "I think that...", "I hope that..." —
and follows the exact same verb-final pattern as yesterday's `weil`, so today reinforces
that pattern on a second connector rather than teaching a new rule. It is also the word
most often confused in writing with `das`, which costs marks in Schreiben even when the
grammar is otherwise correct.

## Core concepts

Same shape as `weil`: inside the `dass`-clause, the verb goes to the very end.

- `Ich glaube, dass es morgen regnet.` (I believe that it will rain tomorrow.)
- `Ich hoffe, dass die Sonne bald scheint.` (I hope that the sun shines soon.)
- `Ich denke, dass es heute windig ist.` (I think that it's windy today.)

`dass` (two s, introduces a fact or clause) and `das` (one s, the article or pronoun for a
neuter noun) sound identical but are never interchangeable in writing — check the
spelling every time you write one.

## Exercises

1. Say "I believe that it will rain tomorrow" using `dass`. — **Hint:** inside a `dass`-clause, the verb goes to the very end, exactly like `weil`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich glaube, dass es morgen regnet.` Check `regnet` is last, not second.
2. Say "I hope that the sun shines soon" using `dass`. — **Hint:** `scheinen` (to shine) still moves to clause-final position inside `dass`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich hoffe, dass die Sonne bald scheint.` Confirm `scheint` is the last word.
3. Write one sentence with `dass` and one with `das`, then check the spelling of each. — **Hint:** `dass` introduces a fact or clause; `das` is the article or pronoun for a neuter noun — they sound identical but are spelled differently. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich denke, dass es heute windig ist. Das Wetter ist schlecht.` Check the first is `dass` (two s) and the second is `das` (one s).

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Putting the verb in second position inside the `dass`-clause, e.g. `dass es regnet morgen` instead of `dass es morgen regnet` — same end-of-clause rule as `weil`, memorise both the same way.
- Confusing `dass` with `das` in writing — they sound identical but are spelled and used differently; check every occurrence before submitting a Schreiben answer.
