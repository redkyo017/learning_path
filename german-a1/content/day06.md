# Day 06 — Regular present-tense endings

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards finish the family theme and start the time theme, and every verb you'll
use to describe your family — `wohnen`, `kommen`, `heißen`, `lernen` — follows the regular pattern that most
German verbs use. `sein` and `haben` were irregular exceptions you had to memorise
whole; today's pattern is a rule you apply to hundreds of verbs for the rest of this
path, so it pays back faster than either irregular verb did.

## Core concepts

Regular verbs add a fixed set of endings to the stem (the infinitive minus `-en`). Take
`wohnen` (stem `wohn-`):

```
ich wohne         wir wohnen
du wohnst         ihr wohnt
er/sie/es wohnt   sie/Sie wohnen
```

This is the [Konjugation](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) pattern: `-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en`.
Apply it to any regular verb — `kommen` → `ich komme, du kommst, er kommt`; `heißen` →
`ich heiße, du heißt, er heißt` (note `du` and `er` land on the same form here, because
the stem already ends in `-ß`). You don't need to explain why — just notice `heißt`
covers both, and move on.

## Exercises

1. Conjugate `wohnen` and `lernen` through all six persons aloud, without looking. — **Hint:** both are fully regular — once you have `wohnen`'s six endings right, `lernen` is the same six endings on a different stem. — **Solution sketch:** `ich wohne, du wohnst, er wohnt, wir wohnen, ihr wohnt, sie wohnen` / `ich lerne, du lernst, er lernt, wir lernen, ihr lernt, sie lernen`.
2. Say one sentence each about your mother and your father using a regular verb, checking that the `er`/`sie` ending lands on `-t`. — **Hint:** the third-person ending is the one most often dropped by Vietnamese-L1 speakers, since Vietnamese verbs never change form for the subject. — **Solution sketch:** `Meine Mutter wohnt in Hanoi. Mein Vater arbeitet in Berlin.`
3. Today's slice (words 76–100) closes theme 02_family and opens 03_time, and carries only four nouns of its own — do all four for article plus plural from memory, then top up to five from yesterday's family words. — **Hint:** family nouns often have irregular plurals with an added umlaut — `Mutter → Mütter`, `Bruder → Brüder` — so don't assume `-e` or `-n` by default. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `der Cousin, die Cousins; die Cousine, die Cousinen; der Ehemann, die Ehemänner; die Ehefrau, die Ehefrauen` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Dropping the `-t` ending on `er`/`sie` sentences — saying `Mein Vater arbeiten in Berlin` instead of `arbeitet`. This is the single most predictable Vietnamese-L1 error named in `STRATEGY.md`, because Vietnamese verbs never inflect at all.
- Filling in the Schreiben Teil 1 Geburtsdatum field in month-first order out of English habit. German forms are always day-month-year — get this automatic today, on a fictional form, before it costs you a real one.
