# Day 12 — Possessives: mein, dein, Ihr

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards finish the food theme and start the health theme, and Block 3 today
drills the exact Teil 2 keyword-card pair for food from `templates/sprechen_scripts.md`. Possessives are the grammar reflex that makes
answers about other people's things sound natural instead of stilted — "my coffee,"
"your bread," "your (formal) order" — and the exam's Teil 2 questions constantly ask
about things that belong to you or your partner.

## Core concepts

`mein` (my), `dein` (your, informal), and `Ihr` (your, formal, always capitalised) all
decline exactly like `ein` — the same masculine change after `haben`-type verbs you
already know, feminine and neuter unchanged.

```
der Kaffee  → mein Kaffee   / Ich habe meinen Kaffee.
die Milch   → deine Milch   / Hast du deine Milch?
das Brot    → Ihr Brot      / Ist das Ihr Brot?
```

A [Possessivpronomen](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) always agrees with the noun it describes, not
with the person who owns it — `mein Bruder` and `meine Schwester` both mean "my," the
ending changes only because `Bruder` is masculine and `Schwester` is feminine.

## Exercises

1. Build three possessive sentences, one each with `mein`, `dein`, `Ihr`, using a masculine noun so the accusative change applies. — **Hint:** the change only shows up when the noun is the object of a verb like `trinken` or `haben` — check whether the sentence needs it before adding the `-en`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich trinke meinen Kaffee. Trinkst du deinen Kaffee? Trinken Sie Ihren Kaffee?`
2. Say the same sentence about a feminine noun for `mein`, `dein`, and `Ihr`, confirming none of them change form. — **Hint:** feminine and neuter possessives never take an `-en` ending in this fixed-chunk pattern — only masculine does. — **Solution sketch:** `Das ist meine Milch. Ist das deine Milch? Ist das Ihre Milch?`
3. From today's new words (words 226–250, closing theme 05_food and opening 06_health), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** most of today's nouns are body parts, and several take an umlaut in the plural — `der Mund → die Münder`, `der Zahn → die Zähne` — so say the plural aloud instead of assuming a plain `-e`. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `das Auge, die Augen; der Zahn, die Zähne; die Nase, die Nasen` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Matching the possessive's ending to the speaker's own gender instead of the noun's gender — a male learner adding no ending to `meine Schwester` because he is male, when the ending depends only on `Schwester` being feminine.
- Using `dein` in a formal or stranger context instead of `Ihr`. Teil 2 examiners are addressed formally; a `dein` slip here reads as impolite, not just grammatically off.
