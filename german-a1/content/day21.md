# Day 21 — können and müssen

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

`können` (can/ability) and `müssen` (must/obligation) are the two most common
[Modalverb](GLOSSARY.md#grammar)en in everyday German, and Sprechen Teil 3 — polite
requests and responses — leans on `können` directly ("Können Sie bitte...?"). Both push
the main verb's infinitive to the very end of the sentence, a word-order pattern that
shows up on every Hören and Lesen module from here on.

## Core concepts

A modal is conjugated in position 2; the main verb stays in its plain infinitive form and
moves to the very end, however much sits between them.

- `Ich kann den Weg nicht finden.` (I can't find the way.)
- `Können Sie mir bitte helfen?` (Can you help me, please?)
- `Ich muss zum Bahnhof gehen.` (I have to go to the station.)
- `Wir müssen ein Taxi nehmen.` (We have to take a taxi.)

Note that `können` changes its stem vowel in the `ich`-form: `können → ich kann`, not
`ich könne`. It is a vowel swap (`ö` → `a`), not simply the two dots falling off —
`müssen → ich muss` and `mögen → ich mag` behave the same way. Memorise each infinitive
with its `ich`-form as a pair.

## Exercises

1. Say "I can't find the way" in German. — **Hint:** `können` conjugates as `ich kann` — the stem vowel changes from `ö` to `a`; memorise the pair `können / ich kann`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich kann den Weg nicht finden.` Check `finden` sits at the very end, after `nicht`.
2. Say "We have to take a taxi." — **Hint:** `müssen` conjugates as `wir müssen`; the infinitive `nehmen` still goes to the end. — **Solution sketch:** `Wir müssen ein Taxi nehmen.` Confirm `müssen` is in position 2 and `nehmen` is last.
3. Turn "Can you help me, please?" into a polite German question. — **Hint:** questions with a modal put the modal first, then the subject, then the infinitive at the end. — **Solution sketch:** `Können Sie mir bitte helfen?` Check `helfen` is the last word.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Putting the infinitive in the wrong place, e.g. `Ich kann finden nicht den Weg` instead of `Ich kann den Weg nicht finden` — the modal is conjugated in position 2 and the infinitive always goes to the very end.
- Conjugating the modal like a regular verb, e.g. saying `ich könne` instead of `ich kann` — `können`'s `ich`-form swaps the stem vowel `ö` for `a`, and that swap is memorised per modal, not derived.
