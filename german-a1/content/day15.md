# Day 15 — es gibt

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 cards sit wholly inside the work theme — health closed yesterday — and `es gibt` ("there is / there are") is a
chunk you'll lean on constantly from here through the travel and city themes ahead —
describing what's available at a clinic, in a town, in a shop, without needing a
specific subject to hang the sentence on.

## Core concepts

`es gibt` is a fixed phrase — literally "it gives" — that never changes form regardless
of what follows, and per the grammar ceiling in `STRATEGY.md` you use it as a chunk
rather than a case rule: the noun after it takes the same masculine change you already
know from `haben`.

```
Es gibt einen Arzt in der Nähe.
Es gibt eine Apotheke im Zentrum.
Es gibt ein Krankenhaus in der Stadt.
```

The phrase itself, `es gibt`, is always exactly these two words — only the article after
it moves, using the same `einen`-for-masculine pattern from day 10.

## Exercises

1. Build three `es gibt` sentences, one with each article gender, applying the change only to the masculine one. — **Hint:** `es gibt` itself never changes — only the article on the noun that follows it can change, and only if that noun is masculine. — **Solution sketch:** `Es gibt einen Supermarkt. Es gibt eine Kirche. Es gibt ein Krankenhaus.`
2. Turn a `haben`-style possession sentence into an `es gibt`-style existence sentence about the same noun. — **Hint:** `haben` says someone owns something; `es gibt` says something exists somewhere — the article rule is identical, but the meaning shifts from possession to availability. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich habe einen Arzt.` (my doctor) → `Es gibt einen Arzt in der Nähe.` (there is a doctor nearby)
3. From today's new words (theme 07_work, words 301–325), pick five nouns and produce article plus plural from memory. — **Hint:** job titles come in matched masculine/feminine pairs — `der Kellner` / `die Kellnerin` — so drill both halves together rather than only the one you would use for yourself. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `der Student, die Studenten; die Kellnerin, die Kellnerinnen; das Projekt, die Projekte` — say each pair aloud twice, then check the wordlist.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Conjugating `gibt` as if it were a normal verb responding to the subject — trying `es gibst` or `es geben` under pressure. `es gibt` is a fixed two-word chunk; the `gibt` never changes regardless of what comes after it.
- Forgetting the masculine article change after `es gibt`, saying `Es gibt ein Supermarkt` instead of `einen Supermarkt` — the same slip from day 10, resurfacing in a new chunk. Treat `es gibt einen …` as one glued unit for masculine nouns, exactly like `Ich habe einen …`.
