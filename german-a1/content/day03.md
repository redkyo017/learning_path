# Day 03 — 21–100 and the reversed digits

**Phase:** 0 — Recon | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

This is the first day you actually study Anki cards — 25 new words from `01_personal`,
the theme that carries your own name, age, country and job, the exact content of the
Teil 1 script you recorded on day 01. It is also the day you meet German's reversed
number order, which governs every price, age and phone number the exam will ever give
you, in every module.

## Core concepts

German counts 21–99 with the ones digit before the tens digit, joined by `und`: 21 is
`einundzwanzig` — literally "one-and-twenty" — never "twenty-one." The pattern holds
through 99: 35 is `fünfunddreißig`, 47 is `siebenundvierzig`, 68 is `achtundsechzig`. The
tens themselves: 20 `zwanzig`, 30 `dreißig`, 40 `vierzig`, 50 `fünfzig`, 60 `sechzig`,
70 `siebzig`, 80 `achtzig`, 90 `neunzig`, 100 `hundert`. This reversed order is the
single most common Hören mistake at A1 — you hear the ones digit first and assume it is
the whole number, missing the tens that follow it.

## Exercises

1. Say 21, 35, 47, 68 and 100 aloud, then check each against the reversed-digit rule above. — **Hint:** say the ones digit first, then `und`, then the tens — the word order is fixed, never the English "tens-then-ones" order. — **Solution sketch:** einundzwanzig, fünfunddreißig, siebenundvierzig, achtundsechzig, hundert. Point at the ones digit as you say the first syllable and the tens digit as you say the last, to physically anchor the order.
2. Read one of your ten recorded phone numbers back using only digit words, then check every `2` was said as `zwei`. — **Hint:** phone numbers are read one digit at a time in German, never grouped into tens like `einundzwanzig` — that pattern is only for standalone numbers; `zwei` is the digit word you use throughout, and `zwo` is a fallback for a mishearing, never your default. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `Meine Nummer ist null eins sieben, drei zwei vier, fünf sechs acht neun.` Replay your recording and mark any `zwo` you said instead of `zwei`; re-record just that digit five times, then the full number once more.
3. Read the Clusters ladder from `templates/pronunciation_drills.md` aloud, then check your recording for an inserted vowel between the consonants. — **Hint:** Vietnamese syllables allow at most one consonant at the start, so the instinct is to slip a short vowel into `spr` or `schw`, producing something like "seprechen." — **Solution sketch:** rebuild the word in steps — "s — sp — spr — sprechen" — then say `Sprichst du im Herbst Deutsch?` and listen for any vowel sound between `s`, `p` and `r`; if you hear one, redo the step-by-step build once more before re-recording the sentence.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Saying numbers in tens-then-ones order — "twenty-one" style — instead of German's ones-then-tens order. This error is doubly reinforced, because Vietnamese also puts the tens first (hai mươi mốt), so both your L1 and English pull you the same wrong direction; German is the outlier here, not you.
- Skipping today's Anki session because "the deck was already set up on day 01." New cards/day was deliberately kept inactive for two days so today's 25 new cards land on a completely empty queue. Skipping today pushes the whole batch to tomorrow and starts a backlog on the very first day of real study.
- Defaulting to `zwo` for every `2` in a phone number. `zwei` is the default in Teil 1 and in the Hören audio; `zwo` is a telephone and radio convention that exists only to stop `zwei` being heard as `drei`, so use it only if your listener actually mishears you — this is exactly what `templates/sprechen_scripts.md` says under Teil 1, Nummer. Switching between the two mid-number is what makes candidates stall on a scored task.
- Skimming `STRATEGY.md` instead of reading it end to end. Today is the only day the full reasoning is required reading — skip it now and you will end up re-deriving the logic mid-plan, on a day when you have no time to spare for it.
