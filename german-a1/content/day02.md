# Day 02 — Buchstabieren and 0–20

**Phase:** 0 — Recon | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 0

## Why this matters

Two skills show up in every Sprechen exam, regardless of which candidate you are:
spelling your own name and saying numbers. Both are tested directly in Teil 1 — the
examiner may ask you to spell your surname, and phone numbers, ages and addresses all
require the numbers 0–20 said instantly, without counting up from one in your head. You
build both today, before any grammar, because both are pure memorisation with no rule to
lean on.

times, increasing speed each pass, and record the fifth.

## Exercises

1. Spell your surname aloud from memory, then check every letter name against the alphabet table in `templates/sprechen_scripts.md`. — **Hint:** the letters that trip up Vietnamese-L1 speakers most are the ones with no English equivalent sound — `ä`, `ö`, `ü`, `ß` — and the pairs that sound close together, `B`/`P` and `G`/`Z`. — **Solution sketch:** for a surname like Nguyen, say "Mein Nachname ist Nguyen. Ich buchstabiere: en, ge, u, ypsilon, e, en." — that is N-G-U-Y-E-N, in that order; say the letters in the order they are written, not in the order they feel natural to you. Slow down and isolate any letter you rushed, then repeat only that letter five times before re-recording the whole name.
2. Count aloud from 0 to 20 without looking at anything written, then check the teens against the written forms. — **Hint:** 16 and 17 are contracted — they drop a letter that the pattern from 13–15 and 18–19 would predict. — **Solution sketch:** null, eins, zwei, drei, vier, fünf, sechs, sieben, acht, neun, zehn, elf, zwölf, dreizehn, vierzehn, fünfzehn, sechzehn, siebzehn, achtzehn, neunzehn, zwanzig — confirm you said `sechzehn` (not `sechszehn`) and `siebzehn` (not `siebenzehn`).
3. Listen back to your `ch` drill recording and compare `ich` with `auch`. — **Hint:** `ich` is made at the hard palate, near the front of the mouth; `auch` is made further back, near the throat — if both sound the same, you are collapsing them into one substitute sound. — **Solution sketch:** say `ich` and freeze your tongue at the moment of the `ch` sound — it should be touching the roof of your mouth near the front teeth, not the back of the throat. Re-record `ich, nicht, Milch` then `auch, Buch, acht` and check the contact point changes.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Spelling with English letter names — saying "double-u" for `W` or "zee" for `Z` instead of the German names from the table. The exam grades Buchstabieren in German, and a habit formed today is hard to unlearn under exam pressure later.
- Writing or saying `sechszehn` and `siebenzehn` for 16 and 17. These are the two most common number errors at this level — drill them by ear now, not by memorising a spelling rule.
