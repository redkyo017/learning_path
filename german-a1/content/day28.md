# Day 28 — Comparatives: gut/besser, gern/lieber

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today's 25 new cards finish the entire Tier 1 deck — all 650 words are in Anki after this
session. The grammar reflex that closes Phase 2 is comparison: `gut/besser` for quality,
`gern/lieber` for preference, both irregular and both essential for Sprechen Teil 2
answers that compare two things ("I like X, but I prefer Y").

## Core concepts

Both pairs are irregular — neither takes the regular "more" pattern you might expect.

- `Mein Deutsch ist gut, aber dein Deutsch ist besser.` (My German is good, but yours is better.)
- `Ich spiele gern Fußball, aber ich schwimme lieber.` (I like playing football, but I prefer swimming.)
- `Ich lese gern, aber ich sehe lieber fern.` (I like reading, but I prefer watching TV.)

`gern` states you like something; `lieber` states which of two things you prefer.
Note `fernsehen` still splits in a normal sentence: `sehe ... fern`.

## Exercises

1. Say "My German is good, but your German is better" using the irregular comparative. — **Hint:** `gut` → `besser` is irregular; never say `mehr gut`. — **Solution sketch:** `Mein Deutsch ist gut, aber dein Deutsch ist besser.` Check you used `besser`, not `mehr gut`.
2. Say "I like playing football, but I prefer swimming" using `gern` and `lieber`. — **Hint:** `gern` states you like something; `lieber` states which of two things you prefer. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich spiele gern Fußball, aber ich schwimme lieber.` Check `lieber` appears only in the second half, marking the preference.
3. Say "I like reading, but I prefer watching TV" using the separable verb `fernsehen`. — **Hint:** `fernsehen` splits in a normal sentence: `sehe ... fern`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich lese gern, aber ich sehe lieber fern.` Check `fern` landed at the very end, separate from `sehe`.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Using `mehr gut` instead of `besser` — `gut/besser` is irregular and does not take the regular "mehr" pattern.
- Using `gern` where a comparison needs `lieber`, e.g. saying `Ich schwimme gern, ich laufe gern` when you mean a preference between the two — that needs `..., aber ich laufe lieber`.
