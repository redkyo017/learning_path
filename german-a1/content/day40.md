# Day 40 — Work and back-to-back modules

**Phase:** 3 — Output | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 32

## Why this matters

The real exam does not give you a rest between Hören and Lesen. Today rehearses that
stamina gap directly, and your monologue opens and closes with small talk instead of
diving straight into content, the way a real conversation does.

## Core concepts

Small talk phrases are not filler — they are what a tutor or a conversation partner hears
first and last, and a garbled one at the very start colours everything that follows. They
are `du`-register phrases, so they belong in the italki calls and in real conversation:
Sprechen Teil 1 opens with the seven-point self-introduction, not with small talk, and
everything said to an examiner stays in the `Sie` register — `Guten Tag.` to open,
`Vielen Dank.` to close. You can also build an opener out of cards you already hold
instead of memorising a new one: `Das Wetter ist heute schön, oder?` is `oder` from
`t2_connectors` plus Tier 1 words, and it keeps the adjective after the verb, where A1
leaves it undeclined.

### Block 2 — Input (40 min)

### Block 3 — Output (40 min)

Speak a 90-second monologue on `07_work`, opening and closing with a `t2_smalltalk`
phrase around the work content in the middle. Draft it once, then deliver and record it
live without reading. Play it back once. Then record the same monologue a second time in
its exam-facing version: `Guten Tag.` to open and `Vielen Dank.` to close, no `du`-register
phrases anywhere in it.

### Block 4 — Exam drill (30 min)

Run Hören then Lesen back to back from the second Übungssatz
(https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/kup/prf/prf/sd1/ueb.html), timed, with only the exam's
normal short gap between them — no extra pause of your own. Score both after the second
one finishes.

## Exercises

1. Open your monologue with a `t2_smalltalk` greeting before the work content. — **Hint:** the greeting is a separate short sentence, not folded into the first work sentence. — **Solution sketch:** `Hallo, wie geht es dir? Ich spreche heute über meine Arbeit. Ich bin [Beruf] von Beruf.` The exam-facing version of the same opening is `Guten Tag. Ich spreche heute über meine Arbeit.`
2. Close your monologue with a `t2_smalltalk` phrase. — **Hint:** a closing phrase signals you are finished, which matters in a timed spoken test where the examiner is listening for an ending. — **Solution sketch:** `Das war's von mir. Bis bald!` for the conversational version; `Das war's von mir. Vielen Dank!` for the exam-facing one.
3. Compare your accuracy at the start of Lesen against the start of Hören, run back to back. — **Hint:** a drop after Hören usually means you need a fixed 10-second reset ritual, not a longer break you won't get on exam day. — **Solution sketch:** write one sentence naming the drop, e.g. `Ich habe bei Lesen Teil 1 zu schnell gelesen.`, and note the fix for next time.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Treating `t2_smalltalk` phrases as filler that doesn't need to be said correctly — a
  garbled `Wie geht es dir?` is the first thing a tutor or a new acquaintance hears from you.
- Carrying these phrases into a scored Sprechen Teil. `Wie geht es dir?` and `Alles klar?`
  are `du`-register, and Teil 1 opens with the self-introduction, not with a small-talk
  exchange — to an examiner it is `Guten Tag.` and `Vielen Dank.`
- Taking an unplanned long break between Hören and Lesen during today's back-to-back
  drill. The real exam gives you a short fixed gap, not a rest whenever you want one.
