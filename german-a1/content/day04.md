# Day 04 — sein, all persons

**Phase:** 1 — Engine | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Today Phase 1 — Engine begins: the fourteen days that build the first half of Nicos
Weg, Wortliste themes 01–08, and a Sprechen Teil 1 you can deliver from memory by day 17. The verb
`sein` ("to be") is the single most common verb in the exam and in your own Teil 1
script — every one of its seven sentences already used a form of it (`Ich bin`, `Ich
bin … von Beruf`), so today you make its full pattern automatic instead of only knowing
the `ich`-form.

## Core concepts

`sein` is irregular — its six forms don't follow the regular pattern you'll meet on day
6, so they simply have to be memorised as a block. This is a
[Konjugation](GLOSSARY.md#grammar): the way a verb's ending (or here, the whole word)
changes for who is doing the action.

```
ich bin        wir sind
du bist        ihr seid
er/sie/es ist  sie/Sie sind
```

Say the six forms as one continuous chant until the order stops needing thought — you
will use `ist` and `sind` constantly from here on, in questions, in descriptions, and in
Teil 2 answers about other people.

### Block 4 — Exam drill (30 min)

Open the Übungssatz audio you downloaded on day 1. Play Hören Teil 1 twice, as the real
paper does — no pausing, no rewinding inside a play, and nothing beyond the second play.
Of the three Hören parts, only Teil 2 is played once; Teil 1 and Teil 3 are played twice.
Note the specific detail each short announcement asks for (a number, a name, a time).
Use the first play for the answer and the second only to confirm it, which is how the
second play is worth marks in the real exam. Check your answers against the
transcript in the PDF, then write down which single detail-type you missed, if any.

## Exercises

1. Say all six forms of `sein` aloud, in order, without looking at the chart. — **Hint:** the pattern breaks in half — `ich bin` / `du bist` / `er ist` share no common ending, but `wir sind` and `sie/Sie sind` are identical. — **Solution sketch:** `ich bin, du bist, er ist, wir sind, ihr seid, sie sind` — say it three times, faster each pass, until it comes out as one breath.
2. Build one true sentence about yourself for `ich`, `du` (asking a friend), and `er/sie` (about someone else), each using a form of `sein`. — **Hint:** the exam constantly needs `sein` to describe people other than yourself, not just `ich bin`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich bin müde. Bist du müde? Meine Schwester ist Studentin.`
3. Pick five nouns from words 1–50 and say article plus plural for each, from memory, then check them against the wordlist. Today's slice (26–50) ends 01_personal and opens 02_family, and carries only three nouns of its own — take those three first, then top up from day 03's words. — **Hint:** Vietnamese has no gender, so guessing the article by logic will fail — you either recall it as a chunk or you don't. — **Solution sketch:** e.g. `die Familie, die Familien; der Vater, die Väter; die Mutter, die Mütter` — read each chunk as one unit, article glued to noun, never the noun alone.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Saying `Ich bin … Jahre alt` correctly (from day 1's script) but freezing on `du bist` or `sie ist` because those forms were never drilled in isolation — Teil 2 constantly asks about other people, so `ich`-only fluency is a trap that feels like competence and isn't.
- Reading the wordlist silently instead of aloud in Block 3. Silent reading builds recognition, not production — see `runbook.md`'s troubleshooting table for what silent shadowing costs you later.
