# Day 20 — Irregular Perfekt, high-frequency

**Phase:** 2 — Expansion | **Total:** 2h15 | **New cards:** 25

## Why this matters

Most of the verbs you actually need every day — go, eat, drink, take, find, meet — are
strong verbs with irregular [Partizip II](GLOSSARY.md#grammar) forms that do not follow
the `ge-...-t` pattern from day 18. There is no shortcut here: each one is memorised
whole, the same way you memorise a noun's article. Today's 25 cards sit wholly inside the
10_routine theme, which supplies the everyday actions these verbs describe.

## Core concepts

Five high-frequency irregular participles, each memorised as one unvarying chunk:

- `gehen` → `Ich bin in die Stadt gegangen.` (sein — motion)
- `nehmen` → `Ich habe den Bus genommen.` (haben)
- `finden` → `Ich habe ein Restaurant gefunden.` (haben)
- `treffen` → `Ich habe meine Freundin getroffen.` (haben)
- `sehen` → `Ich habe das Rathaus gesehen.` (haben)

Only `gehen` takes `sein` here, because it is the only verb of motion in the set — the
other four act on an object and take `haben`, matching day 18's rule.

## Exercises

1. Say "I went into the city" using `gehen` in the Perfekt. — **Hint:** `gehen` is a strong verb and a motion verb, so its participle is irregular and its auxiliary is `sein`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich bin in die Stadt gegangen.` Check you did not write `geht` or `gegeht` — the irregular form is `gegangen`.
2. Say "I found a restaurant" and "I met my friend," then check both participles against the irregular list. — **Hint:** `finden` → `gefunden`, `treffen` → `getroffen` — neither follows the regular `-t` pattern, so both must be memorised whole. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich habe ein Restaurant gefunden. Ich habe meine Freundin getroffen.` Read both aloud and check neither participle ends in `-t`.
3. Build a Perfekt sentence with `nehmen` about taking the bus. — **Hint:** `nehmen`'s participle changes its vowel and ending: `genommen`, not `genehmt` or `genommt`. — **Solution sketch:** `Ich habe den Bus genommen.` Say it aloud twice and check the ending is `-en`, not `-t`.

## Anti-patterns / Common mistakes

- Regularising a strong verb's participle, e.g. saying `geessen` instead of `gegessen`, or `getrinkt` instead of `getrunken` — irregular participles must be memorised whole, not built from a rule.
- Adding a weak-verb `-t` ending to a strong verb, e.g. saying `genommt` instead of `genommen`.
