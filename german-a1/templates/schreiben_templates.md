# Schreiben Templates

Templates for both parts of the Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 Schreiben exam. Never write real personal data into this file — use the bracketed slots when practising.

## Teil 1 — Formular ausfüllen

Teil 1 gives you a short text and a form to fill in with details taken from that text. Learn the exact German field labels below and what belongs in each one.

| Feld | Was hier hineingehört |
|---|---|
| Familienname | Nachname / Zuname (surname) |
| Vorname | Rufname (first name / given name) |
| Straße/Hausnummer | Straßenname, dann die Hausnummer (street name, then house number) |
| Postleitzahl | die fünfstellige PLZ (the five-digit postal code) |
| Wohnort | Stadt oder Ort, in dem man wohnt (the town or city) |
| Geburtsdatum | Tag, Monat, Jahr im Format TT.MM.JJJJ |
| Geburtsort | Stadt, in der man geboren ist (place of birth) |
| Staatsangehörigkeit | z. B. vietnamesisch, deutsch (nationality, as an adjective) |
| Telefon | Telefonnummer, oft mit Vorwahl |
| Unterschrift | von Hand geschrieben, kein Druckbuchstabe |

**Three traps:**

1. **Date order is DD.MM.YYYY.** German dates go day, then month, then year — e.g. 03.11.1998 means 3 November 1998, not March 11. Never write the English month-first order.
2. **Postleitzahl comes before Wohnort.** German forms and addresses put the postal code first, then the town name — e.g. "10115 Berlin," not "Berlin 10115."
3. **Unterschrift is handwritten, not printed.** On a real exam form you sign it by hand; typing or printing your name in that field is wrong even if the letters are correct.

## Teil 2 — Kurze Mitteilung

Teil 2 gives you a short prompt with three points (*Leitpunkte*) you must address. Your message should be about 30 words long and cover all three. Each of the three points is scored separately, so missing one costs you roughly a third of the Teil 2 marks — and Schreiben is 25 of the 100 points you need 60 of. Cover all three, every time. Nothing here is pass/fail on its own: Start Deutsch 1 is one exam scored out of 100, not four modules you can fail individually.

**Skeleton:**

```
Anrede (greeting)
→ Punkt 1
→ Punkt 2
→ Punkt 3
Gruß (closing)
```

**The comma trap.** The greeting takes a comma — `Liebe Anna,` / `Sehr geehrte Frau Müller,` — and the sentence after it starts with a small letter. The closing formula takes **no** comma: `Viele Grüße` and `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` are written bare, with your name on the next line. English and Vietnamese letters put a comma there; German does not, and it is the single easiest mark to give away in this task.

### Worked example — informal (an einen Freund / eine Freundin)

Prompt: cancel a Saturday meeting, propose a new day, ask for a short reply.

```
Liebe Anna,
ich kann am Samstag nicht kommen, weil ich krank bin.
Wir können uns am Sonntag treffen.
Kannst du mir bitte kurz schreiben?
Viele Grüße
[Vorname]
```

Three points covered: (1) reason for cancelling, (2) new proposed day, (3) request for a reply. About 27 words.

### Worked example — formal (an eine Firma / ein Amt)

Prompt: report you cannot keep an appointment, propose a new day, ask for confirmation.

```
Sehr geehrte Frau Müller,
ich habe am Montag einen Termin bei Ihnen.
Ich kann leider nicht kommen, weil ich krank bin.
Geht es am Mittwoch? Bitte bestätigen Sie mir den Termin.
Mit freundlichen Grüßen
[Vorname] [Nachname]
```

Three points covered: (1) you cannot keep the appointment, with the reason — `Ich kann leider nicht kommen, weil ich krank bin.`, (2) a new day proposed — `Geht es am Mittwoch?`, (3) confirmation asked for, in so many words — `Bitte bestätigen Sie mir den Termin.` 34 words.

Point 3 is the one learners drop. Proposing Wednesday is not the same as asking someone to confirm Wednesday, and there is no such thing as a point covered "implicitly" — an examiner scores what is on the page, never what you meant. If the prompt says *ask for confirmation*, then `bestätigen`, or a bare `Geht das?`, has to be written down.

**Rule:** before you submit any Teil 2 message, check it against the prompt's three points one by one. A missing point loses its own marks no matter how good the grammar around it is, so add the missing point first and check grammar second.
