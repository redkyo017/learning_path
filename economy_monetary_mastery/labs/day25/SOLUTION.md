# Solution notes — Lab 25

## momentum_signal
**Idea:** compare today's price with the price `lookback` bars ago; go long (1.0) only if the ratio exceeds 1. **Formula:** `(p / p.shift(lookback) - 1 > 0).astype(float)`. A comparison against NaN is False, so the warm-up bars become 0.0 for free. **Common mistake:** using `rolling(lookback).mean()` of prices (a different signal) or leaving NaN in the output; `test_momentum_signal` expects exact 0/1 values for `[1,2,3,2,1]` with lookback 2.

## backtest
**Idea:** the signal at the close of bar t is only tradable for bar t+1, so `position = signal.shift(1).fillna(0)`. Strategy return is position times that bar's asset return, minus cost on turnover. **Formulas:** `asset_ret = pct_change().fillna(0)`; `turnover = |position.diff()|` with the first row set to `|position[0]|`; `cost = turnover*cost_bps/1e4`; `strat_ret = position*asset_ret - cost`; `equity = (1+strat_ret).cumprod()`. **Common mistakes:** skipping the lag (same-bar execution), which `test_no_lookahead` and the position assertion `[0, 1, 1]` catch; leaving the first turnover row NaN, which turns every later equity value into NaN (`test_costs_charged_on_turnover` expects `[0, 1, 0]`); charging cost in bps without dividing by 1e4.

## What the output means
On the snapshot SPY (2005-01-03 to 2026-09-29) 200-day momentum earns a lower CAGR than buy-and-hold (about 7.6% vs 10.9% before costs) but with about two-thirds of the volatility, a similar Sharpe (0.64 vs 0.64) and a much shallower maximum drawdown (about -34% vs -55%). It is a risk-reduction trade, not a return-enhancing one on SPY. On BTC-USD (annualized with 365 days) it improves Sharpe and drawdown (see the printed table) at about the same CAGR. A 10 bps cost trims CAGR by roughly half a percentage point on SPY because the signal switches about 100 times in 21 years. The vectorbt cross-check, once its entries and exits are moved back one bar to match the lagged position, agrees to four decimals at zero cost and within about 0.02 of final equity at 10 bps (this lab subtracts the cost additively from the next bar's return, while vectorbt deducts it multiplicatively at the fill; the per-entry gap is about c*r + c^2, sign following r).
