# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms used across the 30 days. Each entry ends with the day it is introduced. Facts are as of 2026-10; educational, not financial advice.

## A

**Adjusted close** — A historical price series corrected for splits and dividends, so it rarely equals the price that actually printed on a given day. Use it for returns, not for checking exchange price limits. *(Day 16)*

**Algorithmic stablecoin** — A stablecoin that tries to hold its peg with a mint-and-burn rule and market incentives rather than reserve assets. TerraUSD is the cautionary example: confidence broke and the design spiralled. *(Day 21)*

**AMM (automated market maker)** — A smart contract that holds two tokens in a pool and quotes prices from a formula, so anyone can trade without an order book. Liquidity providers fund the pool and earn fees. *(Day 22)*

**Anchored expectations** — A situation where households and firms believe inflation will return to the central bank's target, so one-off price shocks do not feed into wages and prices. Losing the anchor makes disinflation costly. *(Day 04)*

**Annualized basis** — The futures-minus-spot premium scaled to a yearly percentage so contracts of different maturity can be compared. It is a carry rate, not a forecast. *(Day 23)*

**Ask** — The lowest price at which someone is currently willing to sell. Buyers who want to trade immediately pay it. *(Day 09)*

**ATC (at-the-close)** — The closing auction on the Vietnamese exchanges, where orders are matched at a single price after continuous trading ends. On HOSE it runs 14:30 to 14:45. *(Day 09)*

**ATO (at-the-open)** — The opening auction on HOSE, where orders are matched at a single price before continuous trading starts. It runs 9:00 to 9:15. *(Day 09)*

**Authorized participant (AP)** — A large firm that may create or redeem ETF shares directly with the fund in exchange for the underlying basket. Its arbitrage keeps the ETF price close to NAV. *(Day 09)*

## B

**Backtest** — A simulation of how a trading rule would have performed on past data. It is only as honest as its data, costs and assumptions. *(Day 25)*

**Balance sheet** — A statement of what an entity owns (assets) and owes (liabilities), with the difference being equity. Every day in the course starts from this identity. *(Day 01)*

**Bank run** — A wave of withdrawals triggered by fear that a bank cannot repay everyone. It can turn a merely illiquid bank into an insolvent one. *(Day 02)*

**Base fee** — The part of an Ethereum transaction fee set by the protocol from recent block fullness and burned. It rises when blocks are more than half full and falls when they are emptier. *(Day 20)*

**Basel III** — The international rulebook for bank capital and liquidity, requiring banks to hold more and better-quality capital and enough liquid assets to survive a stress. It limits leverage and runs, not lending directly. *(Day 02)*

**Basis (cross-currency)** — The extra cost or premium, beyond the interest-rate difference, of borrowing one currency against another in the swap market. It measures stress in dollar funding. *(Day 13)*

**Basis (futures)** — The gap between a futures price and the spot price of the same asset. In a cash-and-carry trade, capturing the basis is the payoff. *(Day 23)*

**Basis point** — One hundredth of a percentage point (0.01%). Rates and spreads are quoted in them. *(Day 03)*

**Beta** — How much an asset tends to move when the market moves one unit. A beta of 1.2 means about 20% more movement than the market. *(Day 08)*

**Bid** — The highest price at which someone is currently willing to buy. Sellers who want to trade immediately receive it. *(Day 09)*

**Bid-ask spread** — The gap between the best ask and the best bid. It is the basic cost of trading immediately. *(Day 09)*

**Bitcoin halving** — The scheduled cut of the Bitcoin block subsidy in half every 210,000 blocks, about every four years. The most recent was in April 2024, leaving 3.125 BTC per block. *(Day 19)*

**Blob transaction** — A transaction type that carries a large chunk of temporary data, mainly for rollups, priced in its own fee market. It makes layer-2 data cheaper than ordinary calldata. *(Day 20)*

**Block** — A bundle of transactions plus a header that links to the previous block by its hash. Chained blocks form the ledger. *(Day 19)*

**Block gas limit** — The cap on total gas that all transactions in one Ethereum block may use. As of 2026-10 it is 60,000,000 gas. *(Day 20)*

**Bridge** — A system that moves tokens between blockchains, usually by locking them on one chain and issuing a claim on another. Bridges have been a frequent source of large hacks. *(Day 22)*

**Brier score** — The mean squared difference between probability forecasts and what actually happened (1 or 0). Lower is better, and it punishes confident wrong calls. *(Day 06)*

**Broker** — An intermediary that takes your orders and routes them to an exchange. In the course it also means the API your paper-trading code talks to. *(Day 09)*

**Business cycle** — The recurring rise and fall of output, jobs and credit around a long-run trend. Markets price the expected position in the cycle. *(Day 11)*

## C

**Capital (bank)** — A bank's loss-absorbing cushion: assets minus liabilities. A bank with positive capital is solvent even if it is short of cash. *(Day 02)*

**Capital controls** — Rules that limit how money may move across a country's border. They let a government keep some control over exchange rate and interest rates at once. *(Day 15)*

**Capital market line** — The line of best risk-return trades when you can combine the market portfolio with a risk-free asset. Its slope is the market's Sharpe ratio. *(Day 10)*

**Carry trade** — Borrowing in a low-interest currency to hold a high-interest one, earning the rate gap while hoping the exchange rate does not offset it. It tends to earn steadily and lose sharply. *(Day 13)*

**Cash-and-carry** — Buying the asset and selling a futures contract on it to collect the futures premium. The position is hedged, so the profit is the basis. *(Day 23)*

**CDP (collateralized debt position)** — A smart-contract loan in which you lock collateral and mint a stablecoin against it. If the collateral falls too far, the position is liquidated. *(Day 21)*

**Central bank** — The bank for banks and the government, which issues the economy's base money and sets the policy rate. Examples are the Fed and the State Bank of Vietnam. *(Day 03)*

**Central bank reaction function** — The pattern describing how a central bank changes its policy rate in response to inflation and growth. Markets try to anticipate it. *(Day 11)*

**Central rate** — The daily USD/VND reference rate published by the SBV. Banks may deal only within a band of plus or minus 5% around it. *(Day 16)*

**Centralized exchange (CEX)** — A company-run crypto trading venue that holds customer assets and runs the order book. You trade against its ledger, not the blockchain, and carry its counterparty risk. *(Day 23)*

**CIP (covered interest parity)** — The rule that the forward exchange rate is set so that borrowing in one currency and lending in another earns the same after hedging. It links the forward to interest rates, not to a forecast. *(Day 13)*

**Clearing house (CCP)** — An institution that steps between buyer and seller after a trade so each faces it rather than each other. It guarantees settlement. *(Day 09)*

**Collateral** — An asset pledged to secure a loan, which the lender may keep if the borrower defaults. Repo, DeFi loans and CDPs all rest on it. *(Day 05)*

**Commercial bank** — A bank that takes deposits and makes loans, creating deposit money when it lends. It settles with other banks in central-bank reserves. *(Day 02)*

**Compounding** — Earning returns on past returns, so growth multiplies rather than adds. It is why returns are chained with products, not summed. *(Day 02)*

**Constant-product formula** — The AMM rule x times y equals a constant, which sets the pool price from the token balances. Trading moves the price along a curve. *(Day 22)*

**Consumer price index (CPI)** — A measure of the average change in prices of a fixed basket of consumer goods and services. It is the most-quoted inflation gauge. *(Day 04)*

**Contango** — A market state in which futures trade above spot. The premium is what a cash-and-carry trade collects. *(Day 23)*

**Convexity** — The curvature of the price-yield relationship of a bond, which means duration alone misjudges large rate moves. Positive convexity helps the bondholder in both directions. *(Day 07)*

**Core inflation** — Inflation excluding food and energy, whose prices are volatile. It is used to read the underlying trend. *(Day 04)*

**Correlation** — A number from minus 1 to plus 1 for how closely two returns move together. Diversification works only when correlations are below 1. *(Day 10)*

**Cost-push inflation** — Inflation caused by rising costs of supply, such as energy or wages, rather than strong demand. Raising rates fights it less cleanly. *(Day 04)*

**Coupon** — The fixed interest payment a bond makes, usually yearly or twice a year, quoted as a percentage of face value. *(Day 07)*

**Crawling peg** — An exchange-rate regime in which the official rate is adjusted in small, regular steps. Vietnam runs a managed crawl with a band. *(Day 15)*

**Credit growth quota (room)** — A limit the State Bank of Vietnam sets on how fast each bank may expand lending. It is a quantity tool that works alongside interest rates. *(Day 02)*

**Cross-sectional momentum** — A strategy that buys the recent winners and sells the recent losers among many assets at the same time. It is distinct from time-series momentum, which looks at each asset alone. *(Day 28)*

**Currency board** — A regime that fixes the exchange rate by law and backs the whole monetary base with foreign reserves. Hong Kong is the standard example. *(Day 15)*

**Currency leg** — The part of a foreign investment's return that comes from the exchange rate rather than the asset. Total return in your home currency is asset return times currency return. *(Day 17)*

**Current yield** — A bond's annual coupon divided by its price. It ignores the gain or loss at maturity, unlike yield to maturity. *(Day 07)*

## D

**Data snooping** — Testing many ideas on the same data until one looks good by luck. It inflates every backtest result. *(Day 26)*

**DCF (discounted cash flow)** — Valuing an asset as the present value of its expected future cash flows, discounted at a rate that reflects time and risk. A higher rate means a lower value. *(Day 08)*

**Decentralized exchange (DEX)** — A trading venue made of smart contracts, where you keep custody of your assets until the trade. Many use AMMs. *(Day 23)*

**DeFi** — Decentralized finance: lending, trading and other financial services run by smart contracts on a blockchain. Its risks include bugs, oracles and liquidation cascades. *(Day 22)*

**Deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR)** — The probability that a strategy's Sharpe ratio is genuine after adjusting for how many variants were tried. More trials make the bar higher. *(Day 26)*

**Deposit insurance** — A government-backed guarantee that repays small depositors if a bank fails, which removes their reason to run. In Vietnam it is VND 350 million per depositor per institution since 13 July 2026 (earlier 125 million). *(Day 02)*

**Depth** — The quantity available to trade at or near the best prices in the order book. Thin depth means large orders move the price. *(Day 09)*

**Direct and indirect quote** — A direct quote prices one unit of foreign currency in home currency; an indirect quote does the reverse. Mixing them up flips the sign of a return. *(Day 13)*

**Discount rate** — The rate used to turn future cash flows into present value. It combines the risk-free rate and a risk premium. *(Day 08)*

**Diversification** — Holding assets that do not move together so the portfolio's risk is lower than the average of its parts. It is the one free lunch in finance. *(Day 10)*

**Dividend** — A cash payment a company makes to its shareholders out of profit. It is part of total return but absent from a price index. *(Day 08)*

**Dollarization** — The use of a foreign currency, usually the dollar, for saving or payments in place of the local one. It shrinks the central bank's control. *(Day 15)*

**Dot plot** — The chart in the Fed's quarterly projections showing each policymaker's expected path for the policy rate. It is a set of forecasts, not a promise. *(Day 12)*

**Double entry** — The accounting rule that every transaction changes two entries so both sides of the balance sheet stay equal. T-accounts show it. *(Day 01)*

**Double spending** — Spending the same digital coin twice. Bitcoin's chain of blocks and proof of work exist to prevent it. *(Day 19)*

**Drawdown** — The fall of a portfolio from its previous peak to its later trough, as a percentage. Maximum drawdown is the worst such fall in the sample. *(Day 05)*

**Drawdown brake** — A risk rule that cuts position size when losses from the peak pass a threshold. It limits damage during a bad run. *(Day 27)*

**Duration** — A bond's sensitivity to interest rates, roughly the percentage price change for a one-point rise in yield. Longer maturity and lower coupon mean higher duration. *(Day 05)*

**DXY (dollar index)** — An index of the dollar against a basket of six major currencies, dominated by the euro. It is a common gauge of dollar strength, though not trade-weighted broadly. *(Day 14)*

## E

**Effective federal funds rate (EFFR)** — The volume-weighted median rate on overnight unsecured loans between US banks. It is a market rate the Fed steers with its administered rates, not one it sets directly. *(Day 03)*

**Efficient frontier** — The set of portfolios giving the highest expected return for each level of risk. A portfolio below it can be improved without taking more risk. *(Day 10)*

**Embargo** — A gap left between training and testing data so information cannot leak across the boundary. It is used in time-series cross-validation. *(Day 26)*

**EOA (externally owned account)** — An Ethereum account controlled by a private key, as opposed to a contract account controlled by code. Only EOAs start transactions. *(Day 20)*

**Equity risk premium (ERP)** — The extra return investors require for holding shares instead of a risk-free asset. It is not observed directly and has to be estimated. *(Day 08)*

**ETF (exchange-traded fund)** — A fund whose shares trade on an exchange like a stock and track a basket or index. Authorized participants keep its price near NAV. *(Day 09)*

**Eurodollar** — A US dollar deposit or loan held outside the United States and outside US bank regulation. The market is the offshore core of global dollar funding. *(Day 14)*

**Event study** — An analysis of how prices behave around a dated event, compared with normal behaviour. Its pitfalls are look-ahead, overlapping events and cherry-picking. *(Day 14)*

**Exchange (stock)** — A venue that matches buy and sell orders under published rules. In Vietnam the main ones are HOSE, HNX and UPCoM. *(Day 09)*

**Exorbitant privilege** — The advantage the United States enjoys from issuing the world's reserve currency: cheaper borrowing and a deep market for its debt. The phrase is attributed to Valery Giscard d'Estaing. *(Day 14)*

## F

**Face value** — The amount a bond repays at maturity, also called par. Coupons are quoted as a percentage of it. *(Day 07)*

**Fed funds rate** — The interest rate on overnight lending of reserves between US banks, and by extension the Fed's target range. The effective rate in the market is steered inside that range. *(Day 03)*

**FIMA repo facility** — A Fed facility that lets foreign central banks borrow dollars against US Treasuries without selling them. It acts as a backstop for dollar funding. *(Day 14)*

**Finality** — The point at which a transaction can no longer be reversed. On Bitcoin it is probabilistic; on Ethereum proof of stake it is explicit after finalisation. *(Day 19)*

**Fiscal dominance** — A situation where government debt is so large that the central bank is pressed to keep rates low to help finance it, weakening its inflation control. *(Day 04)*

**Fisher equation** — The relation that the nominal interest rate is about the real rate plus expected inflation. It lets you strip inflation out of a yield. *(Day 04)*

**Fixed fractional sizing** — A position-sizing rule that risks a constant fraction of current capital on each trade. Positions shrink after losses and grow after gains. *(Day 27)*

**Foreign ownership limit (FOL)** — The maximum share of a Vietnamese company that foreign investors may hold in total. When it is full, foreigners can only buy from other foreigners, often at a premium. *(Day 16)*

**Forward points** — The difference between the forward and spot exchange rates, usually quoted in small units. It reflects the interest-rate gap, not a forecast. *(Day 13)*

**Forward rate** — An exchange rate agreed today for exchange at a future date. By CIP it is set by interest rates. *(Day 13)*

**Free float** — An exchange-rate regime in which the market alone sets the rate, with no official target. The central bank rarely intervenes. *(Day 15)*

**Front-running** — Trading ahead of a known pending order to profit from the price move it will cause. In DeFi it is done by bots watching the mempool. *(Day 22)*

**Funding rate** — The periodic payment between longs and shorts on a perpetual future that pulls its price towards spot. A positive rate means longs pay shorts. *(Day 23)*

**FX swap** — A deal that exchanges currencies now and reverses the exchange at a fixed later date. It is the main way dollar funding is borrowed offshore. *(Day 13)*

## G

**Gas** — The unit of computational work on Ethereum. A plain transfer costs 21,000 gas. *(Day 20)*

**Gas price** — The amount of ETH, quoted in gwei, paid per unit of gas. It equals base fee plus tip. *(Day 20)*

**GDP (gross domestic product)** — The total value of goods and services produced in a country in a period. Growth in it defines expansion and recession. *(Day 11)*

**Gordon growth model** — A valuation shortcut in which a payment that grows forever at a constant rate is worth next year's payment divided by the discount rate minus the growth rate. It blows up when the two rates approach each other. *(Day 08)*

**Greenspan-Guidotti rule** — A guideline that a country should hold reserves at least equal to its short-term external debt. It guards against sudden stops of capital. *(Day 15)*

**Gwei** — One billionth of an ETH (10^-9 ETH). Gas prices are quoted in it. *(Day 20)*

## H

**Haircut** — The percentage knocked off a collateral asset's market value when it is pledged. A bigger haircut means less cash lent against the asset. *(Day 05)*

**Hard peg** — An exchange-rate regime that fixes the currency to another with a legal or institutional commitment, such as a currency board. It gives up an independent monetary policy. *(Day 15)*

**Hash** — A fixed-length fingerprint of data produced by a one-way function. Changing any input bit changes the output completely. *(Day 19)*

**Hash rate** — The total number of hash attempts per second made by Bitcoin miners. A higher rate makes an attack costlier. *(Day 19)*

**Hedging (currency)** — Locking in an exchange rate with a forward so the currency leg of a foreign return is removed. It is not free: you pay or earn the interest-rate gap. *(Day 17)*

**Held-to-maturity (HTM)** — An accounting category for bonds a bank intends to hold until they repay, carried at cost, not market value. It hides unrealised losses, as at SVB. *(Day 05)*

## I

**Idiosyncratic risk** — Risk that is specific to one asset and can be diversified away. What remains is market risk. *(Day 10)*

**Impermanent loss** — The shortfall a liquidity provider suffers versus just holding the two tokens when their relative price moves. It becomes permanent when you withdraw. *(Day 22)*

**Import cover** — Foreign reserves expressed in months of imports they could pay for. It is a common yardstick of reserve adequacy. *(Day 15)*

**Impossible trinity** — The rule that a country cannot have a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy all at once. It must give up one corner. *(Day 15)*

**Inflation** — A sustained rise in the general level of prices, which means a falling purchasing power of money. It is measured by an index such as CPI or PCE. *(Day 04)*

**Insolvency** — A state in which a bank's liabilities exceed its assets, so its capital is negative. It differs from illiquidity, which is a shortage of cash. *(Day 02)*

**Interest rate on reserve balances (IORB)** — The rate the Fed pays banks on the reserves they hold at the Fed. It acts as the floor of the policy corridor. *(Day 03)*

**Inversion (yield curve)** — A state in which short-term yields exceed long-term yields. It has often come before recessions, but its timing is unreliable. *(Day 07)*

**Investment policy statement (IPS)** — A written plan stating a strategy's goals, limits and rules before trading starts. The Day 30 capstone requires one. *(Day 30)*

## K

**Kelly criterion** — A formula for the bet size that maximises long-run growth given the odds and edge. Full Kelly is very volatile, so practitioners use a fraction of it. *(Day 27)*

**Kill switch** — A control that halts all trading automatically or on command. It must not be possible to bypass. *(Day 29)*

## L

**Layer 2 (rollup)** — A separate chain that runs transactions off Ethereum and posts the data back to it, reducing fees. It inherits security from Ethereum. *(Day 20)*

**Lender of last resort** — A central bank acting as the source of emergency loans to solvent banks that cannot find funding. It is one of the three public tools against runs. *(Day 02)*

**Leverage** — Using borrowed money or derivatives to hold a position larger than your capital. It magnifies both gains and losses. *(Day 23)*

**Liquidation** — The forced sale of collateral or closing of a position when its value falls below a required level. In DeFi and perps it is automatic. *(Day 22)*

**Liquidity** — How easily an asset can be turned into cash without moving its price. It is high for Treasury bills and low for thinly traded stocks. *(Day 09)*

**Liquidity provider (LP)** — A person who deposits tokens into an AMM pool and earns trading fees in return. LPs bear impermanent loss. *(Day 22)*

**Liquidity risk** — The risk of being unable to meet obligations or sell an asset at fair value when needed. Banks and funds both face it. *(Day 02)*

**Long and short** — A long position gains if the price rises; a short position gains if it falls. Shorting means selling something you have borrowed. *(Day 13)*

**Look-ahead bias** — An error in which a backtest uses information that was not yet available at the time of the decision. It makes results look better than they could be in live trading. *(Day 25)*

**Lot size** — The minimum trading unit. On Vietnamese exchanges it is 100 shares for round-lot orders. *(Day 09)*

## M

**Macaulay duration** — The weighted average time to receive a bond's cash flows, in years. Dividing by one plus the yield gives modified duration. *(Day 07)*

**Managed float** — An exchange-rate regime in which the rate is market-determined but the central bank leans against it from time to time. There is no fixed target. *(Day 15)*

**Marginal price** — The price of the next small unit traded in an AMM, given by the current ratio of the pool's tokens. Large trades get worse prices than the marginal one. *(Day 22)*

**Mark price** — A reference price, usually derived from several spot markets, that an exchange uses for margin and liquidation instead of the last trade. It reduces manipulation. *(Day 23)*

**Market impact** — The movement in price caused by your own trade. It grows with order size relative to depth. *(Day 09)*

**Market maker** — A firm that continuously quotes both bids and asks, earning the spread in exchange for providing liquidity. Its quotes give the book depth. *(Day 09)*

**Market order** — An order to trade immediately at the best available price. It guarantees execution but not the price. *(Day 09)*

**Martingale (betting)** — A scheme of doubling the stake after each loss. It looks safe until a long losing streak or a limit on capital ends it. *(Day 27)*

**Maturity** — The date on which a bond's face value is repaid. Longer maturity means more interest-rate risk. *(Day 07)*

**Maximal extractable value (MEV)** — The profit available to those who can choose, insert or reorder transactions in a block. Sandwiching and arbitrage are common forms. *(Day 22)*

**Mean reversion** — The tendency of a price or spread to move back towards an average. A strategy that bets on it loses when a trend persists. *(Day 28)*

**Mempool** — The waiting area where pending transactions sit before being included in a block. Bots watch it for trading opportunities. *(Day 19)*

**Minimum-variance portfolio** — The combination of assets with the lowest possible volatility. It sits at the left tip of the efficient frontier. *(Day 10)*

**Modified duration** — Macaulay duration divided by one plus the yield, giving the approximate percentage price change per unit change in yield. It is the working version used in the lab. *(Day 07)*

**Momentum** — The tendency for assets that have risen to keep rising for a while, and likewise for falls. It is both a factor and a strategy. *(Day 10)*

**Monetary base** — Currency in circulation plus bank reserves at the central bank. It is the money only the central bank can create. *(Day 01)*

**Money market fund (MMF)** — A fund that holds short-term safe debt such as T-bills and repo, with shares priced near $1. It acts like a bank account but has no deposit insurance. *(Day 05)*

**Money multiplier** — The textbook claim that banks multiply reserves into deposits. In practice loans create deposits, and lending is limited by capital and demand rather than reserves. *(Day 02)*

**Multiple testing** — Running many tests so that some pass by chance. Corrections raise the bar for each result. *(Day 26)*

## N

**NAV (net asset value)** — The per-share value of a fund's holdings. An ETF's price can trade at a premium or discount to it. *(Day 09)*

**Nominal and real** — A nominal value is quoted in current money; a real value is adjusted for inflation. Real rates equal nominal rates minus expected inflation. *(Day 04)*

**Nominal interest rate** — The stated interest rate before adjusting for inflation. The Fisher equation links it to the real rate. *(Day 04)*

**Nonce** — A number miners vary in a block header until the hash meets the difficulty target. Finding it is the work in proof of work. *(Day 19)*

## O

**ON RRP (overnight reverse repo)** — A Fed facility where money funds and others lend cash to the Fed overnight against Treasuries at a fixed rate. It is the effective floor for non-bank cash. *(Day 03)*

**Open-market operations** — Central-bank purchases or sales of securities to change the amount of reserves in the system. They are the main tool for steering short rates. *(Day 03)*

**Optimistic fill** — A simulated trade assuming execution at a better price than a real order would get. It overstates paper-trading results. *(Day 29)*

**Oracle** — A service that feeds outside data, such as prices, into a smart contract. Manipulating it is a classic DeFi attack. *(Day 22)*

**Overfitting** — Tuning a strategy so closely to past data that it captures noise rather than a lasting pattern. It performs well in-sample and poorly afterwards. *(Day 26)*

## P

**Par** — Another word for a bond's face value. A bond trades at par when its price equals face value. *(Day 07)*

**PCE (personal consumption expenditures price index)** — An inflation measure with a broader scope and different weights than CPI. The Fed's 2% target is defined on it. *(Day 04)*

**Perpetual future** — A futures contract with no expiry that stays close to spot through a periodic funding payment. It is the most-traded crypto derivative. *(Day 23)*

**Pips** — The smallest conventional unit of an exchange-rate quote, usually the fourth decimal place. A move of ten pips is 0.0010 for most pairs. *(Day 13)*

**Policy corridor** — The band of rates between the Fed's floor (IORB or ON RRP) and ceiling (primary credit). The market rate lies inside it. *(Day 03)*

**Portfolio** — A collection of assets held together. Its risk depends on how they move relative to each other. *(Day 10)*

**Present value** — Today's worth of money to be received in the future, equal to the amount divided by one plus the discount rate to the power of the time. It is the core of DCF. *(Day 08)*

**Price discovery** — The process by which trading reveals the market's agreed price for an asset. Order books and auctions are its mechanisms. *(Day 09)*

**Price impact** — In an AMM, the gap between the trade's average execution price and the pool's starting price. It grows with trade size relative to the pool. *(Day 22)*

**Price limit** — A daily band outside which a Vietnamese stock cannot trade: plus or minus 7% on HOSE, 10% on HNX, 15% on UPCoM. A locked limit means a quoted price may have no seller or buyer. *(Day 16)*

**Price-time priority** — The rule that the best-priced order matches first, and among equal prices the earliest one wins. Most order books follow it. *(Day 09)*

**Primary dealer** — A bank authorised to trade directly with the Federal Reserve, which supports Treasury auctions. It is a key node in repo markets. *(Day 07)*

**Probabilistic Sharpe ratio (PSR)** — The probability that a strategy's true Sharpe ratio exceeds a benchmark, given the sample length and return shape. It handles estimation error but not multiple trials. *(Day 26)*

**Proof of reserves** — An exchange's demonstration that it holds the assets it says it holds. It does not show liabilities, so it is not proof of solvency. *(Day 23)*

**Proof of solvency** — A demonstration that an exchange's assets exceed all of its liabilities. It is stronger than proof of reserves. *(Day 23)*

**Proof of stake** — A consensus method in which validators lock up coins and are chosen to propose blocks, with penalties for cheating. Ethereum uses it. *(Day 20)*

**Proof of work** — A consensus method in which miners compete to find a hash below a target, spending energy to make rewriting history costly. Bitcoin uses it. *(Day 19)*

**Purchasing power** — The quantity of goods a unit of money can buy. Inflation erodes it. *(Day 04)*

**Purge** — Removing training observations whose time span overlaps the test period. It prevents label leakage in validation. *(Day 26)*

## Q

**QE (quantitative easing)** — A central bank buying long-term securities with newly created reserves to lower long yields and add liquidity. It enlarges the central bank's balance sheet. *(Day 03)*

**QT (quantitative tightening)** — A central bank letting securities mature or selling them so its balance sheet and reserves shrink. In the course the Fed's QT is treated as having ended. *(Day 03)*

## R

**Real interest rate** — The nominal rate minus expected inflation, showing the true cost of borrowing or reward for saving. It can be negative. *(Day 04)*

**Reconciliation** — Checking that your own record of positions and cash matches the broker's. A mismatch is a sign that something has gone wrong. *(Day 29)*

**Reference price** — The prior session's closing matched price, from which daily price limits are measured on HOSE and HNX. *(Day 16)*

**Regime** — A period with a distinct economic pattern, such as growth with falling inflation. Strategies and correlations change across regimes. *(Day 11)*

**Repo (repurchase agreement)** — A short-term loan in which one side sells a security and agrees to buy it back at a higher price. It is secured borrowing and a core funding market. *(Day 05)*

**Reserve currency** — A currency that other countries and institutions hold in large amounts for trade, debt and reserves. The US dollar is the main one. *(Day 14)*

**Reserve requirement** — A rule that banks hold a minimum share of deposits as reserves at the central bank. The SBV still uses it as a tool. *(Day 16)*

**Reserves** — Balances that commercial banks hold at the central bank, used to settle payments between banks. They are the central bank's liability and the banks' asset. *(Day 02)*

**Risk parity** — A portfolio approach that sizes positions so each contributes about the same risk, rather than the same money. It usually holds more of low-volatility assets. *(Day 28)*

**Risk-free rate** — The return on an asset with no default risk, usually a short government bill. It is the base of every discount rate. *(Day 08)*

## S

**Sandwich attack** — An MEV tactic in which a bot buys just before your trade and sells just after, profiting from the price you move. Slippage tolerance limits the damage. *(Day 22)*

**Satoshi** — The smallest unit of bitcoin, one hundred-millionth (10^-8) of a BTC. Amounts on-chain are integers in satoshis. *(Day 19)*

**SBV (State Bank of Vietnam)** — Vietnam's central bank. It runs the central rate, the band, OMO, reserve requirements and credit quotas. *(Day 03)*

**Settlement** — The actual exchange of cash for securities after a trade. The US settles on T+1, Vietnam on T+2 as of 2026-10. *(Day 09)*

**Shadow banking** — Credit and maturity transformation done by institutions that are not insured depositories, such as money funds and broker-dealers. It can run like a bank. *(Day 05)*

**Sharpe ratio** — Average excess return divided by the volatility of returns. It measures return per unit of risk. *(Day 10)*

**Slashing** — A penalty that destroys part of a validator's stake for provable misbehaviour. It makes cheating on proof of stake costly. *(Day 20)*

**Slippage** — The difference between the price you expected and the price you got. It comes from spread, depth and market impact. *(Day 09)*

**Slippage tolerance** — The maximum price worsening a DeFi trader accepts before the swap reverts. Setting it too high invites sandwiching. *(Day 22)*

**Smart contract** — Code stored on a blockchain that runs automatically when called. It cannot be changed easily and can contain bugs. *(Day 20)*

**Solvency** — A state in which assets exceed liabilities. A bank can be solvent but illiquid. *(Day 02)*

**Stablecoin** — A crypto token designed to hold a fixed value, usually one dollar. It may be backed by cash assets, crypto collateral or an algorithm. *(Day 21)*

**Staking** — Locking coins to help secure a proof-of-stake chain in exchange for rewards. The rewards carry slashing and smart-contract risk. *(Day 20)*

**Sterilization** — Central-bank action that offsets the money-supply effect of intervening in currency markets. For example, selling bonds to absorb the reserves created by buying dollars. *(Day 15)*

**Stock (share)** — A unit of ownership in a company, with a claim on its profits. Its value is the discounted stream of expected cash flows. *(Day 08)*

**Stop order** — An order that becomes a market order when the price reaches a trigger. It is not a guarantee of that price. *(Day 09)*

**STRIPS** — Treasury securities with the coupons stripped off, leaving zero-coupon bonds. They show the pure effect of duration. *(Day 07)*

**Subsidy (block)** — The new coins a miner may create in each block, which halves every 210,000 blocks. It is part of the miner's reward along with fees. *(Day 19)*

**Survivorship bias** — The error of testing on only the assets that still exist today, ignoring the ones that failed. It overstates historical returns. *(Day 26)*

**Swap line** — An arrangement in which the Fed lends dollars to a foreign central bank in exchange for its currency. It eases offshore dollar shortages. *(Day 14)*

## T

**T+1** — Settlement one business day after the trade. The US has used it since 28 May 2024. *(Day 09)*

**T+2** — Settlement two business days after the trade. Vietnam uses it as of 2026-10. *(Day 09)*

**T-account** — A simple two-column picture of a balance sheet, with assets on the left and liabilities and equity on the right. It is how the course tracks who owes what. *(Day 01)*

**Tapering** — The Fed slowing the pace of its asset purchases before stopping. The 2013 announcement triggered the taper tantrum. *(Day 14)*

**Taylor rule** — A rule of thumb that links the policy rate to inflation and the output gap. It describes a reaction function. *(Day 11)*

**Term premium** — The extra yield investors demand for holding a long bond instead of rolling short ones. It cannot be observed directly. *(Day 07)*

**Tick size** — The minimum price increment of a stock. On HOSE it is 10 VND below 10,000, 50 VND up to 49,950, and 100 VND from 50,000. *(Day 16)*

**Time-series momentum** — A strategy that goes long an asset when its own past return is positive and short or flat when negative. It is the basis of trend following. *(Day 25)*

**Tip (priority fee)** — The extra payment in an Ethereum transaction that goes to the block proposer. It is set by the sender on top of the base fee. *(Day 20)*

**Total return** — The return including price change and income such as dividends and coupons. A price index leaves the income out. *(Day 17)*

**Total value locked (TVL)** — The value of assets deposited in a DeFi protocol. It measures size, not safety or real usage. *(Day 22)*

**Trading band** — The range around the central rate within which Vietnamese banks may trade dollars: plus or minus 5% since October 2022. The central bank tightens or loosens policy to keep the market inside it. *(Day 16)*

**Transaction costs** — The commissions, spread, slippage and taxes paid to trade. A strategy's breakeven cost is how much it can bear before profit hits zero. *(Day 27)*

**Treasury bill** — A short-term US government debt security issued at a discount and repaid at face value. It is the usual risk-free rate proxy. *(Day 07)*

**Treasury bond** — A long-term US government debt security that pays regular coupons. Its price falls when yields rise. *(Day 07)*

## U

**UIP (uncovered interest parity)** — The theory that a currency with a higher interest rate will depreciate by the rate gap, so carry earns nothing. Empirically it fails on average, which is why carry exists. *(Day 13)*

**Unrealised loss** — A fall in value of an asset still held and not yet sold. Held-to-maturity accounting keeps it off the reported income. *(Day 05)*

**UTXO** — Unspent transaction output: a coin in Bitcoin's ledger that can be spent once, in full, by whoever can sign for it. A balance is the sum of your UTXOs. *(Day 19)*

## V

**Validator** — A participant in proof of stake who locks coins and attests to blocks. It earns rewards for honesty and risks slashing. *(Day 20)*

**Value (factor)** — A tilt towards assets that look cheap against fundamentals. It earns a premium over long periods but suffers long droughts. *(Day 10)*

**Vectorized backtest** — A backtest computed with whole-array operations, not event by event. It is fast but easy to leak the future in. *(Day 25)*

**VN-Index** — The market-capitalisation-weighted index of all stocks on HOSE. It is a price index, so it excludes dividends. *(Day 16)*

**VN30** — The index of the 30 largest and most liquid stocks on HOSE. ETFs such as E1VFVN30 track it. *(Day 09)*

**Volatility** — A measure of how much returns vary, usually the annualised standard deviation. It is the standard proxy for risk. *(Day 10)*

**Volatility targeting** — Scaling a position so that the portfolio's volatility stays near a target. Exposure is cut when markets become volatile and raised when calm. *(Day 27)*

## W

**Walk-forward** — A validation method that repeatedly trains on a past window and tests on the following one, moving forward through time. It respects the arrow of time. *(Day 26)*

**Withholding tax** — Tax taken at source from dividends or interest paid to a foreign holder. It reduces the return before you receive it. *(Day 17)*

## Y

**Yield curve** — A plot of bond yields against maturity. Its shape (normal, flat or inverted) summarises expectations about rates and growth. *(Day 07)*

**Yield to maturity (YTM)** — The single discount rate that equates a bond's price to the present value of its coupons and face value. It is the return if held to maturity and coupons are reinvested at the same rate. *(Day 07)*

## Z

**Zero-coupon bond** — A bond that pays no coupons and is sold at a discount to face value. Its duration equals its maturity. *(Day 07)*
